# Offense and Army Composition

> Classic human-player tactics are examples, not mandatory playbooks. An Agent may be better at commanding mixed forces simultaneously, but should choose combinations that exploit enemy weaknesses rather than mixing units for its own sake.

## 1. Four Checks Before Attacking

1. **Target:** What must be destroyed—Command Center, mining area, turret, factory, or enemy main army?
2. **Counters:** Which nearby enemy units can efficiently damage the planned attacking force? Watch especially for artillery, area damage, anti-air, and Tesla Towers.
3. **Arrival:** Can all units reach the target? Will fast units arrive first and get defeated individually?
4. **Trade:** Is the expected loss worth the gain? Is there a flank, feint, or easier target?

You do not need perfect intelligence before acting, but **after a clear counter-based defeat, do not repeat the same charge under unchanged conditions**.

## 2. Ground Tactics

### Heavy Tanks + Plasma Tanks

- Produced by a T2 Land Factory. Relatively affordable; useful as the core of a mid-tier ground army.
- Common in mass-unit tactics and effective against ordinary ground forces.
- Vulnerable to enemy artillery, experimental tanks, and strong area damage. Do not send dense formations into artillery positions.

### Minigun Mechs

- Produced by a T2 Mech Factory (about 1,000 + 5,000 credits); each unit costs about 4,500+.
- Agile, rapid-firing, and able to cross cliffs. Can overpower many low/mid-tier units and weaker T1/T2 turret defenses.
- **Cannot attack aircraft.** Typically escort with at least two Anti-Air Mechs, adjusting the count to the enemy threat.
- Vulnerable to Tesla Towers, artillery, and Mammoth Tanks.

### Armored Ground Swarms and Mixed Forces

These may include Heavy Tanks, Plasma Tanks, Mammoth Tanks, Minigun Mechs, Flamethrower Mechs, Anti-Air Mechs, Heavy Anti-Air Mechs, and SAM Tanks.

- Produce units relevant to current threats: main damage dealers + anti-air + any needed long-range pressure or shield counters.
- The purpose of a mixed force is **to cover weaknesses**, not maximize the number of unit types.
- **Heavy Hover Tank:** Mid-tier hover unit with balanced stats but relatively low speed; may be useful on mixed land/water routes.

## 3. Air Tactics

| Unit | Suitable tactic | Main risk |
|---|---|---|
| Gunship (about 800) | Mass-produce from a T2 Air Factory; quickly strike bases and buildings with weak anti-air | Low HP; vulnerable to Interceptors, Anti-Air Mechs, and T2/T3 Anti-Air Turrets |
| Amphibious Jet | Electrical damage, relatively high HP, and the ability to submerge and attack surface units; useful in naval contests | Check threats both above and on the water |
| Recon Drone | Fast and relatively durable; scouts, or may absorb some anti-air fire ahead of valuable aircraft | Unarmed; do not sacrifice without a clear benefit |
| Missile Airship | Heavy, high-HP late-game air offense; massed attacks can break defenses when funds allow | Expensive; beware concentrated anti-air |
| AA Fighter | Powerful late-game anti-air aircraft, effective against advanced enemy aircraft | Requires adequate funds and a clear air threat |
| Flying Fortress | Mobile forward base that can produce mid-tier ground forces, or transport troops for deep airdrops | Vulnerable to Interceptors and AA Fighters; verify the route |

In extremely wealthy late-game human matches, dozens of Missile Airships or AA Fighters may appear. This is **not** a default fleet-size requirement.

## 4. Naval and Coastal Offense

**Battleships (about 1,500 credits)** are useful early/mid-game naval units:

- Their range slightly exceeds a T2 Machine Gun Turret's, allowing them to attack lightly defended coasts.
- In favorable conditions, five or more can destroy an isolated Command Center.
- **Cannot attack aircraft.** Use Interceptors or Missile Ships for air/submarine protection when needed.

## 5. Rushes, Raids, and Builder Pushes

| Unit / tactic | Typical purpose | Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Basic Mechs (10+) | Early-game swarm rush | Vulnerable to turrets and area damage |
| Tanks (about 350 each) | Cheap mass attacks against weak positions | Artillery can cause heavy clustered losses |
| Light Gunships (about 250 each) | Produced by a T1 Air Factory; raid exposed enemy Builders | Fragile; not suitable for breaching strong defenses |
| Hover Tanks (about 450 each) | Early raids against positions defended only by T1 turrets | Do not blindly attack upgraded defenses |
| Builders (about 500 each) | Quickly construct turrets and Laser Defense Turrets in groups, push a forward defensive line, or pressure an isolated Command Center | Fragile; require fire support |

"10+" is only a common effective scale in human play, **not a guarantee of victory**.

## 6. Target Priority and Real-Time Coordination

- **Ground attack:** Remove artillery and turrets that directly threaten the ground force. In a time-critical push, purely anti-air structures may be ignored.
- **Air attack:** Account for anti-air defenses and Interceptors first. In urgent situations, ground-only turrets can be bypassed.
- **Rush:** Destroy key units/buildings that can retaliate, or attack the victory-critical target directly when losses are acceptable.
- **Economic warfare:** Resource deposits, exposed Builders, and factories may be more valuable targets than the enemy main army.
- **Long-distance attack:** Interceptors, Heavy Interceptors, transports, and drones often outrun ground forces. Rally at a safe position first when synchronized arrival matters.
- **After a failed push:** Change army composition, target, route, timing, or reconnaissance instead of continuing the same losing plan.

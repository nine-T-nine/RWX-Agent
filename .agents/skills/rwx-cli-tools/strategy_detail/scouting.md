# Scouting and Intelligence Reliability

> Scouting should **reduce uncertainty about a specific action**, not permanently reveal the entire map. Current observations take priority over older reports. Losing vision does not mean the enemy has disappeared.

## 1. When to Scout

| Situation | What to confirm | Action after confirmation |
|---|---|---|
| Preparing a major frontal attack | Artillery, T2/T3 defenses, area damage, anti-air, enemy positions | Choose a breakthrough route and counter-composition |
| Previous attack collapsed immediately | Which enemies caused most losses, and whether they have repositioned | Change tactics instead of repeating the assault |
| Many unobserved neutral resource deposits | Which deposits the enemy controls and whether Builders are exposed | Expand, raid, or deny enemy expansion |
| Friendly base attacked by enemies outside vision | Direction, approach route, and attacking unit types | Defend, pursue, or build a fallback line |
| Preparing an airdrop or amphibious assault | Enemy anti-air and interception threats over water or in the air | Choose a viable route and timing |

## 2. Common Reconnaissance Units and Structures

- **Spy Drone (about 1,500 credits):** Produced by a T2 Air Factory. Relatively high HP; suited to dedicated scouting.
- **Watchtower T1 (about 1,500 credits):** Extends fixed-area vision, often from just behind the frontline.
- **Watchtower T2 (about 2,000 additional credits):** Covers more fog of war. Standard Builders cannot construct these directly; verify the available construction method.
- **Interceptors / Heavy Interceptors:** Fast enough to confirm targets quickly, but keep them out of effective enemy anti-air range.
- **Other fast aircraft:** May scout when needed. Decide whether risking one is justified by the intelligence gained.

## 3. Reasoning Under Fog of War

Separate information into three classes:

1. **Currently visible:** Can guide immediate tactics, but can change during combat.
2. **Last known:** The unit may remain nearby, but its exact location and count are now uncertain.
3. **Unscouted:** Absence of enemy markers is not evidence that the area is safe.

Retain threat assumptions for last-known artillery, anti-air, and enemy main forces. If those assumptions determine whether a costly attack or investment will succeed, scout again when practical.

## 4. Do Not Let Scouting Delay Decisions

- Act when available evidence supports a low-risk move.
- Scout before risky attacks, but do not wait indefinitely for perfect information.
- Repeated scouting is worthwhile only if new information could change an order or strategy.
- Recon units, especially fast aircraft, are not free disposable assets.
